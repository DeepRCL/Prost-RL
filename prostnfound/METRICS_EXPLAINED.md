- These metrics use a **fixed threshold (likely 0.5)**
- At this threshold, the model might be:
  - **Very conservative** → Classifying everything as benign
  - **Outputting probabilities below 0.5** for most cases
  
### Why This Happens:

2. **Conservative calibration**: Model was trained/calibrated to avoid false positives

### Why It's Still GOOD:

- **High sensitivity at low specificity** proves the model CAN detect cancer

## 📊 How the ROI and CLS Percentages Work

### In Your Heatmap Visualizations:

**ROI (Region of Interest) - e.g., "ROI: 45%"**
```
This means: The average predicted cancer probability 
in the needle region is 45%
```
- Source: Decoder heatmap averaged over needle mask
- Range: 0% (no cancer predicted) to 100% (high cancer probability)
- **>40% = High prediction** (model thinks there might be cancer here)

**CLS (Classification) - e.g., "CLS: 67%"**
```
This means: The classification head predicts a 67% 
probability of clinically significant prostate cancer (csPCa)
```
- Source: Image-level classification head
- Range: 0% (benign) to 100% (csPCa)
- **>40% = High prediction** (model thinks this is likely csPCa)
